Unit 5a — Quantum mechanics

Grades 10–11 · Quantum
When classical physics breaks
Classical physics predicts that a hot object should radiate infinite energy. It doesn't. Planck's fix was desperate — he assumed energy comes in discrete chunks. Einstein confirmed it with the photoelectric effect. Classical physics is broken. What replaces it?
Concept — the photon as an interaction

If you think of a photon as the same thing as an electromagnetic wave, you have to imagine each photon as an ever-expanding bubble of field, spreading outward from its source. The intensity at any single point on that bubble can be much, much lower than the energy of a single photon.

The field intensity at a given point does not give you the photon's energy directly. It gives you the probability that matter at that point will interact with the field. When the interaction happens, the electron receives a definite quantum of energy — the whole photon's worth, delivered at once. And the entire surrounding field collapses instantaneously. All of the energy that was spread across the bubble vanishes everywhere except at the point of interaction.

This is why "wave or particle?" is the wrong question. The wave is real and spread out — it tells you the probability landscape. The particle is the interaction event — that is where the energy is actually delivered.

Try this — make the abstract concrete

Calculate your own mass from quarks

Take your own body weight. Estimate how many atoms are in it. Count the quarks and electrons inside those atoms. Multiply by the known masses of up quarks, down quarks, and electrons. Check whether the total adds up.

It will not. Most of your mass comes not from the quarks themselves but from the binding energy of the strong force holding them together — E = mc² in action. The exercise teaches three things at once:

  • Powers of ten — Avogadro's number, atom counts, the scale jumps between an atom and a body.
  • Unit conversion — eV/c², kg, grams, moles.
  • The deep result — about 99% of your mass is binding energy, not particles.

A starter outline:

  • A 70 kg body is roughly how many moles of atoms? How many atoms total?
  • Each atom has Z protons, N neutrons, Z electrons. Mostly hydrogen, carbon, oxygen.
  • Each proton and neutron is three quarks. Up quark ≈ 2.2 MeV/c². Down quark ≈ 4.7 MeV/c².
  • Add it up. Compare to 70 kg. The gap is where the binding energy lives.

Unit 5b — Relativity

Grade 11 · Relativity
What "fixed relative to what?" did to physics
Maxwell's equations give a fixed speed for light. Fixed relative to what? Einstein took it seriously — the speed of light is the same for everyone, always. This breaks time, space, and mass as we understood them.
