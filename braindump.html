Three Pillars of Science ▶
  • 1 · Resources — wealth, time, sponsorship. Smart people exist everywhere. What's rare: smart people with leisure. Medici, Roman patronage, modern universities
  • 2 · Mental resources — curiosity, abstraction, ideology, discrete thinking, natural science boundary. Intelligence ≠ better problem solving. Curiosity does — it makes you investigate opposing ideas

The Natural Science Boundary — verbatim ▶

Discrete vs Continuous thinking ▶
  • Continuous thinking: infinite gradation, no smallest part
  • Discrete thinking: everything from smallest indivisible units — "atoms of ideas"
  • Find the atoms → build everything else up from them
  • Materials once thought continuous — until atomism. Fields still continuous (Rovelli)
  • Same shift applies to thinking itself: find atomic ideas, compose upward
  • Related: spherical cow approximation (Carroll) — idealizations, generalizations, approximations
  • Related: clockwork universe — state now predicts future. Was "describing → predicting" a real historical mental shift?

Framework
Why no earlier Scientific Revolution — Harari + Carroll ▶
  • Harari: No civilization before ~1500 admitted ignorance. Sacred texts claimed the important things were already known. Scientific Revolution = revolution of ignorance, not knowledge
  • Carroll (math): You can't do modern physics without tools to write and measure it. Algebra (820 CE), calculus (1665), telescope (1608), pendulum clock (1657)
  • Two phases: Descriptive (1500–1665) — find patterns without deriving them. Unifying (1665+) — Newton shows Kepler's 3 laws follow from one force equation
  • Key insight: Math was sufficient by 1300. The bottleneck was cultural — printing press + Reformation + colonial funding converged 1450–1520
  • Carroll (criticality): Sandpile model — pile self-organizes to critical slope. One grain can trigger nothing or a total avalanche. Science crossed into self-organized criticality ~1665
  • Islamic Golden Age = most poignant near-miss. Had ~70% of ingredients. Mongol sack of Baghdad (1258) removed grains, pile relaxed back to subcritical
Core thesis

The tipping point isn't a year — it's the first moment all ingredients are simultaneously strong. Harari explains what gets grains onto the pile. Carroll explains what happens once criticality is reached.

Solid
Key example
Kerala school — the sharpest argument in the framework ▶
Madhava of Sangamagrama developed infinite series for sin, cos, arctan — including what we now call the Taylor series — around 1400 CE, roughly 250 years before Newton and Leibniz.
  • It didn't cascade — no printing press, no empire-funding loop, no critical density of other ingredients
  • Work stayed regional, written in Sanskrit and Malayalam, nearly lost to history
  • Better question than "why did the revolution happen in Europe?" → "why did Kerala NOT trigger one in India?"
  • Proves: math is necessary but not sufficient. The social / criticality arguments do the real work
Solid
Math teaching
Matrix intuition — column view vs row view ▶
  • Row view (textbook): dot each row with the input vector. Came from solving equations — each row IS one equation. This is where the convention crystallized (Cayley, 1858)
  • Column view (deeper): each column is a "food profile" or basis direction. Input vector = weights on those columns. Output = weighted sum of columns
  • Both give identical results. Column view is conceptually richer
  • 3Blue1Brown: always think of M·x as linear combination of columns — HIGH RELIABILITY
  • The inverse nutrient problem (negative yogurt) shows matrices don't just compute — they reveal when your question is unsolvable in your current space. Same geometry as eigenvectors
Session 2 · Pedagogy
The Deep Forest approach
Solid
Core pedagogy · Verbatim
The Deep Forest — verbatim monologue ▶
"You go into the deep forest, and you see amazing things, and you try to understand very complicated things. And you can get the idea. You don't need specifics. You can get the idea. You can answer some big questions, and you're trying to find kind of safe spots in the deep, dark forest. And with the deep forest, you don't have to dumb it down. You can show people that there's quantum mechanics and special relativity and that it changes things, but you don't have to explain it all — which is the problem with modern school curriculum is that they're so oriented towards exams that they're afraid to even talk about anything unless you can completely describe it and explain it. Which leaves the student with a lot of information that is not explained and contradictive. And so it leaves only one option, which is to memorize everything, which is not very interesting, which is why students don't want to learn."
  • Even Grade 5 students should encounter quark stars, Planck stars, wave-particle duality
  • The inspiring destination is the motivation to learn the prerequisites
  • Exams are fine — they test specific things, as they should. But they are the wrong starting point
  • Language analogy (Krashen): grammar rules are learned after thousands of hours of exposure, not before. Fluency is automatic and intuitive — not rule-retrieved
  • Physics analogy: learn what matter is and how forces work first. Then ask the specific mathematical question. Context makes the precision memorable
Solid
Math pedagogy · Verbatim
Math as intuition first — verbatim monologue ▶
"Multiplication is not natural on a line. If you take two meters times two meters, you get four meters squared — that makes sense, you stretch it twice. But if you measure the same thing in millimeters: two thousand times two thousand gives four million. On a line that number becomes enormous. As a square it looks the same — a natural two-by-two. The smaller your units, the more infinitely long the number becomes on the line. So multiplication is only symmetrical in planes, not on lines."
"When you try to find the solutions to a quadratic equation, they give you the determinant. They don't explain where it comes from. So I've had a lot of fun trying to reinvent the determinant. What I discovered was completing the square — which connects Pascal's triangle, the two-added-constant-squared, and the derivative of the quadratic function. You connect all of those things and you see why they're useful to one another."
  • Multiplication is naturally 2D — forcing it onto a line breaks intuition and creates unit-dependent nonsense
  • Quadratic formula should be derived by completing the square, not memorized
  • Pascal's triangle appears in binomial expansion — never mentioned in school, but it unifies many things
  • Derivative of x² as area growing on two sides simultaneously — 2x falls out geometrically, no rule needed
  • What is raising to a power? Can be decomposed into rotations and stretching
  • Zero and infinity: circular vs linear geometry show their relationship differently
Solid
Pedagogy
Memorization — its role and its limits ▶
  • Memorization works — Anki, spaced repetition, used heavily by doctors and Japanese learners
  • Long-term retention comes from natural re-exposure in the field, not artificial drilling
  • Memorization creates hooks — initial anchors that let you recognize things when you encounter them
  • Without context, rote memorization creates hooks that attach to nothing and decay fast
  • With context (the deep forest), the same hook attaches to a rich web — and becomes permanent
  • Krashen's "noticing": you can't hear Japanese pitch accent until you have a hook for it. Once you do, you notice it everywhere. If it has no use, it dies. If it has use, natural exposure sustains it
  • The long-term solution is richer contextual analysis — natural hooks — not better memorization tools

Session 2 · Curriculum Structure
Science history as a discovery chain
Solid
Curriculum spine
Five discovery chains — the historical spine ▶
Belongs in the curriculum map — move when finalized. Math thread is not yet threaded through this chain.
1
Gases → mass conservation → atomic theory — Lavoisier, Dalton. Phlogiston collapses when you weigh the products. Matter doesn't appear or vanish.
2
Telescope + conservation of momentum → Newton's laws — Kepler's data + Galileo's experiments + Newton's calculus. Descriptive laws become derivable consequences.
3
Light + electrical tools → EM unification — Faraday, Maxwell. Four equations about electricity and magnetism. Out falls the speed of light. Light was not mentioned.
4
Tools + chemistry → particles, atom model — Cathode rays, Rutherford scattering, Bohr model. The atom has internal structure.
5
Electron + light → relativity + quantum mechanics — Photoelectric effect, blackbody crisis, de Broglie, Schrödinger. Classical physics breaks completely.
Missing thread

Math is not yet mapped onto this chain. Each step required new math to be invented or adopted. That dependency needs to be made explicit — algebra, logarithms, calculus, complex numbers, linear algebra each appear at specific points.
