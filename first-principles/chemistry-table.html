Chemistry's pre-Mendeleev mess

1600 → 1900 · the messy phase before the periodic table organised everything

In 1600, chemistry was almost entirely alchemy. Four classical elements (earth, water, air, fire) sat alongside the three principles of Paracelsus (mercury, sulphur, salt). Phlogiston, the fire-stuff released during burning, was about to be proposed. Vitalism said organic compounds needed a living soul to form. The luminiferous aether filled all of space. Caloric was the substance of heat. A tin of recipes and metaphors, very little law.

By 1900, almost all of that had been replaced. Conservation of mass killed phlogiston (Lavoisier, 1789). Wöhler's urea synthesis killed vitalism (1828). Kinetic theory killed caloric (1850s). Michelson-Morley killed the aether (1887). Mendeleev's periodic table (1869) organised the elements into a single deductive structure. Mendel quietly opened genetics (1865). Maxwell unified electricity and magnetism (1865). The 300-year arc below — same proportional Gantt as the physics page, same phase colouring, same X markers — shows how the cleanup happened.

The cleanup before the cleanup

Modern physics is in roughly the state chemistry was in around 1820 — a few clean foundations, a lot of partly-understood phenomena, several still-active theories that may turn out to be the next phlogiston. Cosmologists today still talk about "dark matter" the way 18th-century chemists talked about phlogiston, as a stand-in for something that has to be there but cannot yet be named. The pattern is older than physics.

What this chart shows by analogy is that the mess clears. Phlogiston lasted about 100 years from Becher to Lavoisier. Caloric lasted about 70 years from Lavoisier himself to Joule and Clausius. Vitalism lasted from the Renaissance to a single afternoon in Wöhler's lab. The luminiferous aether outlasted them all, surviving from Huygens to Michelson-Morley — over 200 years — before two experimentalists made one careful interferometer.

The modern physics table is the same chart with the dates shifted by 300 years. The supersymmetric particles, the WIMPs, the strings, the multiverse — at least one of those is almost certainly the new phlogiston. We don't yet know which.
