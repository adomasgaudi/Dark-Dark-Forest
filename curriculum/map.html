First Principles

The spine of this curriculum follows science history as a chain of discoveries — each unlocked by a new tool or observation. Students encounter the inspiring questions first and work backwards to the precision those questions demand.

"You go into the deep forest and see amazing things. You don't need to understand everything to keep going. Only after you have intuition do you ask the specific questions exams demand — and then memorization has context."

Mathematics · Grade 8 → 9 exam
▶ Topic I · Numbers
  • Natural numbers and integers (including negative numbers and operations on them)
  • Divisibility rules for 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 9, 10
  • Prime and composite numbers; prime factorisation
  • Greatest common divisor (GCD) and least common multiple (LCM)
  • Fractions: proper, improper, mixed; add, subtract, multiply, divide
  • Decimals: place value, conversion to and from fractions
  • Ratios and proportions; direct and inverse proportionality
  • Percentages: of a number, increase and decrease, simple interest
  • Powers with positive integer exponents and laws of exponents
  • Square roots and cube roots; perfect squares
  • Scientific notation for very large or very small numbers
▶ Topic II · Algebra
  • Variables, algebraic expressions, evaluation
  • Like terms and simplification
  • Polynomials: terms, degree, classification (monomial, binomial, trinomial)
  • Polynomial operations: addition, subtraction, multiplication
  • Special products: (a+b)², (a−b)², (a+b)(a−b)
  • Factoring: common factor, grouping, special patterns (difference of squares, perfect square trinomial)
  • Linear equations in one variable: solving and checking
  • Linear inequalities: solving and graphing on a number line
  • Systems of two linear equations: substitution and elimination
  • Word problems: motion (d = v·t), mixture, age, money
▶ Topic III · Geometry
  • Angle types: acute, right, obtuse, straight, reflex; complementary, supplementary, vertical
  • Parallel lines cut by a transversal: corresponding, alternate interior, alternate exterior, co-interior angles
  • Triangles by sides: scalene, isosceles, equilateral
  • Triangles by angles: acute, right, obtuse
  • Triangle angle sum = 180°; exterior angle theorem
  • Congruence criteria: SSS, SAS, ASA, AAS, RHS
  • Pythagorean theorem and its converse
  • Perimeter and area: rectangle, square, parallelogram, triangle, trapezoid, rhombus
  • Quadrilateral properties and classification
  • Circle: radius, diameter, chord, circumference, area
  • 3D solids: cube, cuboid, prism, pyramid, cylinder, cone, sphere
  • Surface area and volume of the above solids
▶ Topic IV · Combinatorics
  • Fundamental counting principle (multiplication rule)
  • Addition rule for mutually exclusive cases
  • Tree diagrams for systematic counting
  • Simple permutations and arrangements
  • Simple combinations
  • Basic factorial notation
Limited to simple problems at this level.
Physics · Grade 8 → 9 exam
▶ Topic I · Structure of matter
  • Atomic notation: chemical symbol with mass number A on top, atomic number Z on bottom
  • Atomic number Z = number of protons (defines the element)
  • Mass number A = protons + neutrons
  • Isotopes: same Z, different A (e.g. ¹²C, ¹³C, ¹⁴C)
  • Alpha decay (α): emits a helium nucleus; A −4, Z −2
  • Beta-minus decay (β⁻): neutron → proton + electron; A unchanged, Z +1
  • Beta-plus decay (β⁺): proton → neutron + positron; A unchanged, Z −1
  • Gamma emission (γ): high-energy photon; A and Z unchanged
  • Half-life: time for half a radioactive sample to decay
  • Nuclear fission: heavy nucleus splits (uranium-235, chain reaction)
  • Nuclear fusion: light nuclei combine into heavier ones (hydrogen → helium in stars)
  • Conservation of atomic and mass numbers in nuclear equations
  • Radiation protection: distance, time, shielding (paper for α, aluminium for β, lead for γ)
Basically: what atoms are made of and how they break apart.
▶ Topic II · The Universe
  • Hierarchy of cosmic objects: planet, moon, star, planetary system, star cluster, nebula, galaxy, galaxy cluster
  • The Solar System: Sun + 8 planets, moons, asteroid belt
  • Nebulae as stellar nurseries: emission, dark, planetary, supernova remnants
  • Stellar energy source: nuclear fusion of hydrogen into helium (proton-proton chain)
  • Low-mass stars (~Sun): main sequence → red giant → planetary nebula → white dwarf
  • High-mass stars (≥8 solar masses): main sequence → red supergiant → supernova → neutron star or black hole
  • Galaxy types: spiral (Milky Way), elliptical, irregular
  • Light-year as a unit of distance
  • The Big Bang and the expanding universe (brief introduction)
Surprisingly deep for a Grade 8 exam — they need to know how stars live and die.
▶ Topic III · Electricity
  • Electric charge: positive and negative, conservation, unit (coulomb, C)
  • Electric current: rate of charge flow, I = Q/t, unit (ampere, A)
  • Voltage / potential difference: energy per charge, V = W/Q, unit (volt, V)
  • Resistance: opposition to current, R = V/I, unit (ohm, Ω) — Ohm's law
  • Electrical work: W = V·I·t, unit (joule, J)
  • Electrical power: P = V·I, unit (watt, W); kilowatt-hour (kWh) for energy bills
  • Series circuits: current the same throughout; voltages add; resistances add
  • Parallel circuits: voltage the same across branches; currents add; 1/R = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂
  • Reading circuit diagrams: symbols for battery, resistor, switch, bulb, ammeter, voltmeter
  • Unit conversions: mA↔A, mV↔V, kΩ↔Ω, kWh↔J

Chain 4 · Grades 9–10
Tools + chemistry → particles, atom model
Grades 9–10
Thomson fires electrons through a vacuum tube and finds something smaller than an atom. Rutherford fires alpha particles at gold foil — most go straight through, a few bounce back. The atom is almost entirely empty space. What is inside?
Physics Cathode rays and the discovery of the electron · Rutherford scattering experiment · Bohr model: electrons in quantized orbits · Why atoms don't collapse — the stability problem · Spectral lines as a fingerprint of atoms
Chemistry Periodic table derived from atomic structure · Electron shells and chemical bonding · MO theory: bonding vs antibonding orbitals · Why H₂ forms but He₂ doesn't · Why O₂ is paramagnetic
History of Science Vacuum tubes and cathode rays as enabling tools · Thomson, Rutherford, Bohr — three models in 20 years · How chemistry and physics were forced into the same room
Math Matrices as operators · Eigenvectors — intuition first · Complex numbers: full treatment

Chain 5 · Grades 10–12
Electron + light → relativity + quantum mechanics
Grades 10–11 · Quantum
Classical physics predicts that a hot object should radiate infinite energy. It doesn't. Planck's fix was desperate — he assumed energy comes in discrete chunks. Einstein confirmed it with the photoelectric effect. Classical physics is broken. What replaces it?
Physics Blackbody radiation: the ultraviolet catastrophe · Photoelectric effect: light as particles · De Broglie: matter as waves · Schrödinger equation — qualitative · Heisenberg uncertainty principle · Pauli exclusion from antisymmetry · Spinors: the electron needs 720° to return to its original state · Stern-Gerlach experiment
Chemistry Electron orbitals: why those shapes · Periodic table as quantum consequence · Why noble gases don't bond · Nodes as rotations in the complex plane
Math Euler's formula e^iθ = cosθ + i sinθ · Imaginary numbers as physically real rotations · Matrices as operators on state vectors
● Antibonding → spinors full path ○ Blackbody curve visualizer ○ Double-slit simulator
Grade 11 · Relativity
Maxwell's equations give a fixed speed for light. Fixed relative to what? Einstein took it seriously — the speed of light is the same for everyone, always. This breaks time, space, and mass as we understood them.
Physics The problem with Maxwell + Newtonian mechanics · Speed of light: invariant and why this matters · Time dilation · Length contraction · E = mc²: derivation sketch · General relativity: gravity as spacetime curvature
Math Spacetime interval: the invariant quantity · Lorentz transformation · 4-vectors
○ Spacetime diagram ○ Twin paradox visualizer
Grades 11–12 · Human Biology
A protein is a chain of atoms. Its shape determines its function. For 50 years nobody could predict the shape from the sequence alone. AI just solved it — using physics.
Human Biology DNA: information stored in chemistry · Proteins: amino acids and folding · Enzymes as molecular machines · Cell membrane as selective barrier · ATP and the mitochondria
Chemistry Organic chemistry: carbon's versatility · Hydrogen bonds in biological structures · Acids, bases and pH in the body · Drug-receptor binding as molecular geometry
