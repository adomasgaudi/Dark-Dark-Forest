The method

Intuition first.
Precision second.
Evidence-based throughout.

The curriculum is built on what learning research actually says — and on Krashen's insight that fluency precedes rules, applied to physics instead of language.

01
Deep forest first
Students encounter the inspiring question — quark stars, EM unification, quantum weirdness — before the precision is demanded. The deep destination is the motivation to learn the prerequisites. Krashen's Input Hypothesis applied to science.
02
Derive, don't memorize
Every "magic formula" has a picture underneath. The quadratic formula falls out of completing the square. The derivative of x² falls out of area growing on two sides. The picture is the curriculum; the formula is the souvenir.
03
One coherent story
Science as a chain of discoveries, each unlocked by a new tool or observation. Topics aren't a checklist — they're causally linked. The student always knows why this comes after that.
04
Context creates retention
Memorization without context decays. With deep-forest context, the same hook attaches to a rich web and persists. The fix for poor retention is richer context, not better flashcards.
05
English-language sources
The best science explainers — Feynman, Carroll, 3Blue1Brown, Welch Labs, Veritasium — are in English. Designing in translation handicaps the curriculum before it starts.
