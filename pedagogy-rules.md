# Pedagogy — rules

Dense reference. One line per rule, category, or anti-pattern. No prose. Expanded reasoning lives in [pedagogy.md](pedagogy.md).

---

## Core

- Methodology is **sprinkled** into content, never **gated** before it.
- Students never have to read methodology to begin learning.
- Give them what they came for. Sprinkle in what I want them to have, unavoidably.

---

## Eleven teaching moves

1. **Practical question first** — open with what the student needs to answer, not history or philosophy.
2. **Philosophy of science, sprinkled** — embed at the moment a derivation makes a leap.
3. **History as a tool** — walk the path that built the answer. Never a standalone side quest.
4. **Math integrated with physics and chemistry** — math arrives when the physical problem needs it.
5. **Cross-connect formulas** — surface shared patterns (inverse-square, logarithmic units, distance/time symmetries).
6. **Don't trust your knowledge** — repeated meta-lesson. Memorisation ≠ understanding. Quality-of-science ≠ quality-of-information.
7. **Iceberg learning** — tag every explanation by tier.
8. **Evidence + consensus markers** — tag every claim by what supports it and by how many scientists accept it.
9. **Sharpen imagination with exact numbers** — every hypothetical or experiment ships with concrete parameters. "Imagine an electron at 0.99 c", not "a fast electron". Math is what lets imagination move to the next level.
10. **Memory techniques** — sprinkle inline. Natural first (cross-links, history, causal structure); unnatural when needed (palaces, Dominic system, vivid imagery). Never a separate course — always tied to the specific fact in front of the student.
11. **Spaced repetition + input/output, passive/active** — re-encounter at growing intervals (Anki-family). Distinguish *passive input* (reading a derivation) from *active output* (deriving it with the page closed). For now: structure facts and exercises with hooks so a future SR engine can scrape them; engine itself is deferred.

## Iceberg tiers

Every explanation carries a tier marker. Shallow = common + less accurate. Deep = rare + more accurate.

| Tier | Label | Audience profile |
|------|-------|------------------|
| 1 | **Layman** | Default-wrong intuitions everyone has. 
| 2 | **Beginner** | Pop-science reader. Correct vocabulary, shallow grasp. |
| 3 | **Highschool** | Typical high schooler with slightly above-average grades. Textbook-correct, no machinery. |
| 4 | **Undergraduate** | First-year university. Methods + simple derivations. |
| 5 | **Graduate** | Advanced specialised study. Full mathematical machinery for one branch. |
| 6 | **PhD** | Research depth in a sub-field. |
| 7 | **Frontier** | Open questions. Edge of the known. |
| 8 | **Nobel** | New fundamentals. Top of the world. |

- **Holy trio** = simplicity + accuracy + clarity in one. Usually via a single analogy. Rare. Pursue when possible.
- Tag the tier explicitly so the student knows whether they are reading a first approximation or the actual mechanism.

## Evidence + consensus markers

Every meaningful claim carries two tags. Both, not one.

**Kind of support**:

- **Experimentally proven** — repeatable experiments confirm.
- **Theoretically derived** — follows from established theory.
- **Logically derived** — follows by deduction from first principles.
- **Theoretical / proposed** — internally consistent, not yet confirmed.

**Consensus** (among active researchers in the field whose work passes peer review — *not* laypeople or journalists):

| Marker | % accept | Read as |
|--------|----------|---------|
| ★★★★★ | ~99% | Overwhelming consensus |
| ★★★★ | ~80% | Strong majority |
| ★★★ | ~50% | Contested |
| ★★ | ~20% | Minority |
| ★ | <5% | Fringe / speculative |

Public consensus ≠ scientific consensus. Marker tracks the scientific side only.

The two tags are independent. *Experimentally proven* claims can be ★★★★★ (Big Bang) or rarer; *theoretical / proposed* claims can still hit ★★★★ (cosmic inflation) when the framework is mathematically clean and indirectly supported.

## Anti-patterns

- Methodology gating the content.
- History as a standalone side quest.
- Math taught separately from the physics or chemistry that motivates it.
- Top-of-iceberg explanation with no tier marker — student mistakes it for the truth.
- Memorisation framed as understanding.
- Top-down lecturing about *how to learn*.
- "It's not just X, it's Y" framing.
- Throat-clearing openers and AI-tells (delve, tapestry, realm, leverage, seamlessly).
- Semicolons in student-facing prose.

## Style rules (from voice.md, summarised)

- Voice equation: Levi's weight + Feynman's joy + Borges's restraint.
- No semicolons in prose or v2 rewrites — they read as an AI tell. Use commas, periods, em-dashes.
- "Rearrange" ≠ "rewrite" — Pass 2 preserves the writer's exact words and only restructures.
- Edit at one level at a time (L1 Surface → L2 Structure → L3 Voice → L4 Conceptual). See [editing-guide.md](editing-guide.md).
